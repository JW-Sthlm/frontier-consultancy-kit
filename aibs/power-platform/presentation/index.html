The review lens

Four principles.
Four ways to fail honestly.

SOFTWARE IN DAYS

The next thing they can run

A working agent, flow or app in a governed environment each loop.

Fails as: a loop ending in a status deck.

CLIENT IN LOOP

A counterpart deciding now

One named business decider, a written maximum wait and escalation.

Fails as: business involvement only at steering meetings.

EVIDENCE FIRST

The latest fact, not belief

Investigate real estate, permissions and documents before designing.

Fails as: assuming what is running or published.

TRANSFER BY DESIGN

The next client-owned action

The receiving owner runs the harness on their own machine.

Fails as: a wiki and a client still calling you.

What HVE should help you specify and review

Guide development.
Ask for the right evidence.

01 QualifyFrame the outcome

Ask HVE which business decision, baseline and owner make a first slice worth developing.

02 DiscoverInvestigate evidence

Supply relevant records and ask for facts, assumptions, missing context and verified capability claims.

03 FrameCompare designs

Challenge alternatives, scope and constraints before accepting component choices.

04 ProveSpecify a test

Ask what a bounded experiment could establish, and what would disprove the recommendation.

05 BuildGuide implementation

Obtain exact behavior, data/action contracts, ordered tasks and dependencies. A separate implementer executes them.

06 AssureReview results

Bring feedback against accepted tests. Separate supplied scenarios, actual observations and unexecuted recommendations.

07 OperateDiagnose and recover

Ask HVE to reason from failure evidence, preserve uncertainty and define the next safe change.

08 TransferContinue without rescue

Let another person find accepted project evidence and request the next bounded development task.

09 CompoundReuse the method

Extract a named question set, contract or test pattern. Replace client facts and revalidate assumptions.

The full HVE method includes implementation. This course focuses on upstream feature/schema decisions; coding capability is not the core task. Five teaching requests do not certify all nine stages or prove a platform operation.

Building a Copilot Studio agent, the HVE way

Let the tools do the work.
Keep the decisions with a person.

SOFTWARE IN DAYS

Answer one real question in a governed environment this loop.

Not only a topic map or demo script.
CLIENT IN LOOP

Name the person who accepts the agent's actual answers.

Not only a steering summary.
EVIDENCE FIRST

Use sources that exist under the permissions that actually apply.

Not what somebody believes is published.
TRANSFER BY DESIGN

The receiving owner reruns the set and ships a small change while you watch.

Not a handover deck.
The squad resolves stages, holds the gate and keeps the record. People retain authority.

Four decisions, recorded in the constraint register with an owner

  1. General knowledge?

    May it answer beyond the approved sources?

  2. Knowledge + tools?

    Which sources and actions may it reach, as which identity?

  3. Unattended + customer-facing?

    What may run without review, and who may interact with it?

  4. What is right?

    What tests define acceptance, and who reruns them?

Success criteria

What would show
this method did not work?

Six months later, the second engagement took the same effort as the first, and nothing from the first was reused.

Collect the evidence now

  • Effort and elapsed time before work starts
  • Gates that changed a decision, not meetings held
  • Named artefacts carried into engagement two
  • Time to the first governed production agent
  • The date an owner ran the harness unaided

Conditions that invalidate the approach

  • No named business decision owner
  • No governed environment obtainable
  • No observable, disconfirmable outcome
  • An engagement shorter than a full loop
  • No receiving owner with time to take over

One disconfirming sentence sits above two evidence columns. Test the delivery method with the same discipline used to test the product.
